The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) is a significant welfare scheme in India. 

It was enacted in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The scheme aims to provide at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The primary objective is to improve the purchasing power of rural households. 

It also aims to create durable assets in rural areas. 

The scheme seeks to enhance livelihood security and reduce rural poverty. 

Under MGNREGA, the government guarantees employment to every rural household. 

The benefits include a legal guarantee for at least 100 days of wage employment. 

Wages are paid at the minimum wage rate as determined by the state government. 

The scheme also promotes social inclusion by providing jobs to marginalized communities. 

Women are encouraged to participate as they are entitled to one-third of the total jobs. 

Eligible beneficiaries include all rural households. 

Any adult member of a rural household can apply for employment under the scheme. 

The application process involves submitting a written request for work to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The Gram Panchayat is required to provide employment within 15 days of the application. 

If employment is not provided within this period, the applicant is entitled to an unemployment allowance. 

The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development. 

State governments are responsible for the execution of the scheme at the local level. 

The implementation involves the Gram Panchayat and the Block Development Officer. 

The scheme is funded by the central government and state governments in a cost-sharing ratio. 

The central government bears the majority of the wage costs.
