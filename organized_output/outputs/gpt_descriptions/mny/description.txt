The MNY scheme stands for the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act. 

It was launched in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The primary objective is to provide at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme aims to improve the livelihood of rural households and create durable assets in rural areas. 

Benefits include guaranteed wage employment and the creation of community assets. 

Wages are paid at the minimum wage rate as per the state's schedule. 

Eligibility is limited to adult members of rural households. 

The application process involves submitting a job card application at the local Gram Panchayat. 

The timeline for employment is based on demand and can be accessed throughout the year. 

The implementing authorities include the Ministry of Rural Development and local Gram Panchayats. 

Additional details include provisions for worksite facilities and social audits to ensure transparency.
