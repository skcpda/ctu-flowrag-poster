The Integrated Scheme for Street Children is a comprehensive initiative launched by the Government of India. 

This scheme aims to address the issues faced by street children across the country. 

Street children are often vulnerable to various forms of exploitation and abuse. 

They lack access to basic necessities such as food, shelter, education, and healthcare. 

The government recognized the need for a structured approach to support these children. 

The Integrated Scheme for Street Children was introduced to provide a holistic framework for their welfare. 

The primary objective of the scheme is to ensure the survival and development of street children. 

It aims to provide them with access to education, healthcare, and social services. 

The scheme also focuses on the rehabilitation and reintegration of street children into mainstream society. 

It seeks to empower these children by providing them with life skills and vocational training. 

The benefits of the scheme are multi-faceted. 

Street children are provided with food and shelter through various facilities established under the scheme. 

They receive access to healthcare services, including vaccinations and regular health check-ups. 

Educational support is a significant component of the scheme. 

Street children are enrolled in formal or non-formal education systems. 

The scheme also provides scholarships to encourage their continued education. 

Counseling and psychological support are offered to help them cope with their circumstances. 

Vocational training programs are available to equip them with skills for employment. 

The scheme also emphasizes the importance of family reunification wherever possible. 

It aims to provide support to families to prevent children from living on the streets. 

Eligibility for the scheme is primarily based on the status of the child. 

Children who are living on the streets or in difficult circumstances are eligible for assistance. 

There are no specific age restrictions mentioned in the scheme. 

However, the focus is primarily on children below the age of 18 years. 

The application process for the Integrated Scheme for Street Children is designed to be accessible. 

Local authorities and non-governmental organizations play a crucial role in identifying eligible children. 

Applications can be submitted through these organizations or directly to local government offices. 

The process typically involves filling out a form that details the child's circumstances. 

Documentation may be required to verify the child's status as a street child. 

Once the application is submitted, local authorities assess the situation. 

The timeline for processing applications can vary based on local conditions. 

However, efforts are made to expedite the process to ensure timely support. 

The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Women and Child Development. 

State governments and local bodies are also involved in executing the scheme at the grassroots level. 

Non-governmental organizations often partner with the government to provide services. 

These organizations bring expertise and resources to support the implementation of the scheme. 

Monitoring and evaluation mechanisms are in place to assess the effectiveness of the scheme. 

Regular reports are generated to track the progress and impact of the initiative. 

Feedback from beneficiaries is also collected to improve services. 

The scheme is part of a broader national policy framework for child welfare. 

It aligns with international commitments to protect the rights of children. 

The Integrated Scheme for Street Children is a significant step towards addressing child poverty. 

It aims to create a supportive environment for street children to thrive. 

Through this scheme, the government seeks to reduce the number of children living on the streets.
