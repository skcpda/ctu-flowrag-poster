The National Urban Livelihoods Mission (NULM) is a government initiative aimed at reducing poverty in urban areas. 

It was launched in September 2013 under the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs. 

The primary objective is to provide employment and promote self-employment among the urban poor. 

The scheme aims to enhance the livelihood opportunities of the urban poor through skill development and financial inclusion. 

Benefits include skill training for the urban poor and access to credit for self-employment ventures. 

The scheme also provides support for the establishment of self-help groups and promotes entrepreneurship. 

Eligibility is generally limited to individuals from urban poor households. 

The application process typically involves registration with the local urban local body or designated agencies. 

The timeline for skill training programs varies based on the specific training provider and course duration. 

Implementing authorities include the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs and state governments. 

Additional details include the provision of financial assistance for setting up self-employment ventures and the promotion of market linkages for products created by self-help groups.
