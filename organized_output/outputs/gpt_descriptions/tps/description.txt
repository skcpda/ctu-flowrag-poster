The "TPS" scheme refers to the "Targeted Public Distribution System" in India. 

It was introduced to provide subsidized food grains to the underprivileged sections of society. 

The main objective is to ensure food security for the poor and to reduce hunger and malnutrition. 

The scheme provides essential commodities such as rice wheat and sugar at subsidized rates. 

Eligible beneficiaries include families below the poverty line and those identified through the socio-economic caste census. 

The application process typically involves submitting a form to the local ration shop or government office. 

The distribution of food grains occurs monthly. 

The implementing authority is the Ministry of Consumer Affairs Food and Public Distribution. 

State governments also play a crucial role in the implementation of the scheme. 

The scheme is monitored through a network of fair price shops across the country. 

Beneficiaries receive a ration card which is essential for availing of the benefits.
