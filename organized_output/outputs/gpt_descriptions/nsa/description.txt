The National Security Act (NSA) is a preventive detention law enacted in India. 

It was established to provide for preventive detention in certain cases. 

The primary objective of the NSA is to ensure the security of the nation and maintain public order. 

The Act allows the government to detain individuals without trial for a period of up to 12 months. 

Benefits include the ability to prevent activities that may threaten national security or public order. 

The NSA can be invoked against individuals who are deemed a threat to the security of the state. 

Eligibility for detention under the NSA includes individuals involved in activities that may disrupt public peace or security. 

The application process involves the authorities issuing a detention order based on the perceived threat posed by the individual. 

The detention order must be communicated to the detained individual as soon as possible. 

The implementing authorities include state governments and law enforcement agencies. 

The NSA is applicable across India and can be enforced by state governments as per their discretion. 

The Act provides for a review of detention orders by an advisory board. 

The advisory board consists of persons who are or have been, or are qualified to be appointed as judges of a High Court. 

The NSA has been subject to criticism and debate regarding its implications for civil liberties.
