The scheme is known as the Stand-Up India Scheme (SSBS). 

It was launched by the Government of India in April 2016. 

The context of the scheme is to promote entrepreneurship among Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and women. 

The primary objective is to facilitate bank loans between 10 lakh and 1 crore for setting up greenfield enterprises. 

The scheme aims to provide financial support to at least two such enterprises per bank branch. 

Benefits include access to credit and support in the form of handholding and mentoring. 

Eligible beneficiaries are individuals from Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and women above the age of 18. 

The application process involves approaching a bank branch with a detailed project report. 

The timeline for loan disbursement varies based on the bank's internal processes. 

The implementing authorities include the Ministry of Finance and various public sector banks. 

The scheme also encourages the establishment of enterprises in manufacturing, services, and trading sectors. 

Training and skill development programs are also part of the support provided under the scheme. 

The scheme promotes self-employment and job creation in the economy. 

Monitoring and evaluation are conducted by the respective banks and the Ministry of Finance.
