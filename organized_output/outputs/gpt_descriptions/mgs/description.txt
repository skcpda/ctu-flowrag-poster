Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) is a significant welfare scheme in India. 

It was enacted in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The primary objective is to provide at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme aims to create durable assets and improve the livelihood security of rural households. 

Under MGNREGA, beneficiaries receive a daily wage for the work they perform. 

The wage rate is determined by the state government and is revised periodically. 

Eligibility for the scheme includes all rural households. 

Households must apply for a job card to avail of the benefits. 

The application process involves submitting an application to the local Gram Panchayat. 

Once the application is approved, a job card is issued. 

The job card serves as proof of entitlement to work under the scheme. 

The timeline for wage payments is generally within 15 days of the completion of work. 

The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development. 

State governments are responsible for the execution at the local level. 

MGNREGA also promotes social inclusion by providing work to marginalized sections of society. 

Regular monitoring and evaluation are conducted to ensure transparency and accountability. 

The scheme has provisions for grievance redressal to address any issues faced by beneficiaries.
