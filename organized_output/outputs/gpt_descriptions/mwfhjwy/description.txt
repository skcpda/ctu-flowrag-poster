The scheme "Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act" is commonly referred to as MGNREGA. 

It was enacted in 2005 to provide legal guarantee for at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The primary objective is to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The scheme aims to create durable assets and strengthen the livelihood resource base of rural households. 

Benefits include the provision of employment to rural households and the creation of infrastructure in rural areas. 

Wages are paid at the minimum wage rate as determined by the state government. 

Eligibility is limited to adult members of rural households who are willing to do unskilled manual work. 

The application process involves submitting a job card application to the local Gram Panchayat. 

Once the job card is issued, beneficiaries can request work under the scheme. 

The timeline for employment is within 15 days of the request for work. 

The Ministry of Rural Development is the implementing authority for this scheme. 

State governments are responsible for the implementation at the state level. 

The scheme is funded by the central government and state governments in a cost-sharing ratio. 

MGNREGA also promotes social inclusion by providing employment to marginalized sections of society. 

Regular monitoring and evaluation are conducted to ensure transparency and accountability.
