The National Food Security Act is a government welfare scheme in India. 

It was enacted in 2013 to provide subsidized food grains to the population. 

The objective is to ensure food security for the people by providing access to adequate food at affordable prices. 

The scheme aims to improve nutritional levels among the vulnerable sections of society. 

Under the scheme, eligible households receive food grains at highly subsidized rates. 

The benefits include 5 kg of food grains per person per month. 

The scheme covers rice wheat and coarse grains. 

Eligibility is based on the identification of priority households by state governments. 

The scheme primarily targets Below Poverty Line families. 

The application process involves submitting an application to the local authorities for inclusion in the priority household list. 

The timeline for the distribution of food grains is monthly. 

The implementing authorities include the Ministry of Consumer Affairs Food and Public Distribution. 

State governments are responsible for the implementation at the state level. 

The scheme also includes provisions for the nutritional support of pregnant and lactating women and children. 

It provides for the establishment of a grievance redressal mechanism. 

The scheme is a crucial part of India's strategy to combat hunger and malnutrition.
