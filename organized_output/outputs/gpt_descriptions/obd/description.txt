The OBD scheme stands for the One District One Product scheme. 

It was launched by the Government of India to promote indigenous products. 

The objective is to encourage local artisans and small businesses. 

The scheme aims to enhance product quality and marketability. 

It seeks to create job opportunities in rural and semi-urban areas. 

The benefits include financial assistance for production and marketing. 

It provides support for skill development and training for artisans. 

Eligibility criteria include being a resident of the district. 

Small businesses and artisans engaged in traditional crafts can apply. 

The application process typically involves submitting a proposal to the district authority. 

The timeline for approval may vary based on the district's processing capabilities. 

The Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises is the implementing authority. 

State governments also play a role in the implementation of the scheme. 

The scheme encourages the development of a unique product for each district. 

This helps in promoting local culture and heritage. 

It also aims to increase exports of regional products.
