The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act is a government welfare scheme in India. 

It was enacted in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The primary objective is to provide at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme aims to improve the livelihood security of rural households and create durable assets in rural areas. 

Eligible beneficiaries include every rural household that requests employment under the scheme. 

The application process involves submitting a written request for work to the local Gram Panchayat. 

Employment is provided within 15 days of the request. 

The implementing authority for this scheme is the Ministry of Rural Development at the central level. 

State governments and local bodies are responsible for the execution at the state and local levels. 

The scheme also focuses on the empowerment of marginalized communities and women. 

Wages are paid according to the minimum wage rates set by the state government. 

The scheme has provisions for grievance redressal to ensure accountability. 

It also encourages the creation of community assets and infrastructure in rural areas.
