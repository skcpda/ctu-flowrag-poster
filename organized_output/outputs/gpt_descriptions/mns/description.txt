The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme is a government initiative aimed at enhancing livelihood security in rural areas. 

It was launched on September 7 2005. 

The primary objective is to provide at least 100 days of guaranteed wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme aims to create durable assets in rural areas and strengthen the livelihood resource base of the rural poor. 

Benefits include the provision of unskilled manual work at minimum wage rates. 

The scheme also promotes social inclusion and empowerment of marginalized communities. 

Eligibility is limited to every rural household that applies for work under the scheme. 

The application process involves submitting a demand for work to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The work is provided within 15 days of the application. 

The implementing authority is the Ministry of Rural Development at the central level. 

State governments and local self-governments are responsible for the execution of the scheme. 

The scheme is funded by the central government and state governments in a 75:25 ratio. 

The scheme is monitored through a decentralized structure involving local bodies. 

Regular audits and social audits are conducted to ensure transparency and accountability.
