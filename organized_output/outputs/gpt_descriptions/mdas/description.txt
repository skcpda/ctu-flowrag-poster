The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act is a significant welfare scheme in India. 

It was enacted in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The primary objective is to provide at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme aims to improve livelihood security and create durable assets in rural areas. 

Benefits include guaranteed wage employment and the creation of community assets. 

The assistance provided is in the form of wages for unskilled manual work. 

Eligibility is limited to adult members of rural households. 

Households must apply for a job card to avail of the benefits. 

The application process involves submitting a request to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development. 

Wages are paid according to the schedule set by the state government. 

The scheme is operational throughout the year. 

The implementing authorities include the Gram Panchayat and the District Rural Development Agency. 

Regular monitoring and evaluation are conducted to ensure effective implementation.
