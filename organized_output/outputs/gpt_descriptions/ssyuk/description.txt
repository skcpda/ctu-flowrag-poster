The Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) is a government savings scheme for the girl child in India. 

It was launched as part of the Beti Bachao Beti Padhao initiative. 

The primary objective is to promote savings for the education and marriage of the girl child. 

The scheme encourages parents to invest in the future of their daughters. 

The benefits include a high interest rate compared to regular savings accounts. 

The current interest rate is set by the government and is reviewed quarterly. 

Deposits made under this scheme are eligible for tax deductions under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act. 

The account can be opened with a minimum deposit of Rs. 250 and a maximum of Rs. 1.5 lakh per financial year. 

The account can be opened in the name of a girl child aged below 10 years. 

Parents or legal guardians can open the account. 

The account can be maintained until the girl turns 21 years old. 

Partial withdrawals are allowed for education purposes after the girl turns 18. 

The application process involves filling out a form and submitting it at designated banks or post offices. 

Required documents include the birth certificate of the girl child and identity proof of the guardian. 

The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Finance. 

The interest is compounded annually. 

The account can be transferred from one bank or post office to another. 

The scheme aims to ensure financial security for the girl child. 

It also promotes the importance of education for girls.
