The LBS (Lal Bahadur Shastri) National Academy of Administration is not a welfare scheme but an institution for training civil servants in India. 

The academy is located in Mussoorie, Uttarakhand. 

It was established in 1959. 

The primary objective of the academy is to enhance the skills and knowledge of Indian Administrative Service (IAS) officers. 

The academy conducts various training programs for civil servants. 

These programs include foundation courses for newly recruited IAS officers. 

The training focuses on developing leadership qualities and administrative skills. 

The academy aims to instill a sense of commitment to public service among trainees. 

The training curriculum includes subjects such as public administration, governance, and law. 

The academy also emphasizes the importance of ethical governance. 

It provides a platform for interaction among civil servants from various states. 

The benefits of the training include improved efficiency in governance. 

Trainees gain insights into the functioning of the Indian government. 

They also learn about the socio-economic challenges faced by the country. 

The eligibility to attend the training programs is primarily for IAS officers. 

Newly recruited IAS officers undergo a mandatory foundation course. 

The application process for training is managed by the Union Public Service Commission. 

Once selected through the UPSC examination, candidates are allotted to the academy. 

The training timeline varies depending on the specific program. 

The foundation course typically lasts for around 15 weeks. 

Subsequent training programs may vary in duration. 

The academy is governed by the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions. 

It operates under the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances.
