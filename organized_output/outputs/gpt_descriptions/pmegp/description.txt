The Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) is a credit-linked subsidy scheme. 

It was launched in 2008 by the Government of India. 

The scheme aims to promote self-employment among youth and create sustainable employment opportunities. 

It provides financial assistance for setting up new micro-enterprises in the manufacturing and service sectors. 

The primary objective is to generate self-employment and promote entrepreneurship. 

The benefits include a subsidy on the project cost and access to bank credit. 

The subsidy ranges from 15% to 35% depending on the category of the beneficiary and the location of the project. 

The maximum project cost eligible for subsidy is Rs. 25 lakh for manufacturing units and Rs. 10 lakh for service units. 

Eligibility criteria include being an Indian citizen aged between 18 and 55 years. 

The applicant must not have any existing business or have availed of any other subsidy scheme. 

The application process involves submitting a project proposal to the nearest bank or through the online portal. 

The proposal should include details of the project, estimated cost, and expected outcomes. 

The timeline for approval varies but generally takes a few weeks after submission. 

The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME). 

State governments and banks also play a role in the implementation and monitoring of the scheme. 

The scheme is reviewed periodically to assess its impact and effectiveness.
