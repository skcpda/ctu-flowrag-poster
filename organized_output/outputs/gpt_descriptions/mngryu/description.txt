The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) is a significant welfare scheme in India. 

It was enacted in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The primary objective is to provide at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme aims to improve rural infrastructure and empower rural communities. 

Benefits include guaranteed wage employment and the creation of durable assets in rural areas. 

The assistance is provided in the form of wages for unskilled manual work. 

Eligibility is limited to adult members of rural households. 

Households must apply for a job card to be eligible for employment under the scheme. 

The application process involves submitting an application to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The timeline for wage payment is generally within 15 days of the work being completed. 

The Ministry of Rural Development is the primary implementing authority for MGNREGA. 

State governments are responsible for the implementation at the state level. 

The scheme also promotes social inclusion and gender equality by providing equal wages to men and women. 

MGNREGA has provisions for grievance redressal to address issues faced by beneficiaries. 

The scheme is funded by the central government with contributions from state governments. 

Regular monitoring and evaluation are conducted to assess the impact and effectiveness of the scheme.
