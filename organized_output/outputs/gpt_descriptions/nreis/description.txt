The National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme (NREGS) is a flagship program of the Government of India. 

It was launched in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The primary objective is to provide at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme aims to improve the purchasing power of rural households and enhance their livelihood security. 

Benefits include guaranteed wage employment and the creation of durable assets in rural areas. 

The assistance provided is in the form of wages for unskilled manual work. 

Eligibility is limited to rural households that apply for work under the scheme. 

All adult members of the household can apply for employment under NREGS. 

The application process involves submitting a demand for work to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The Gram Panchayat is responsible for issuing a job card to eligible households. 

The timeline for employment is typically within 15 days of the application for work. 

The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development at the national level. 

State governments are responsible for the execution and monitoring of the scheme. 

NREGS also emphasizes the participation of women and marginalized communities. 

The scheme has provisions for grievance redressal to address issues faced by beneficiaries. 

Regular audits and social audits are conducted to ensure transparency and accountability. 

The scheme is funded by the central government and state governments in a cost-sharing ratio. 

NREGS has played a significant role in reducing rural poverty and improving living standards.
