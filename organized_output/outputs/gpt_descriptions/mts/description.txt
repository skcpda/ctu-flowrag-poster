The MTS scheme refers to the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act. 

It was enacted in 2005 to provide a legal guarantee for at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The objective is to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The scheme aims to create durable assets and strengthen the livelihood resource base of rural households. 

Benefits include wage payments for unskilled manual work. 

The scheme also promotes social inclusion and empowerment of marginalized groups. 

Eligibility is limited to adult members of rural households. 

Households must apply for a job card to avail of the benefits. 

The application process involves submitting an application to the Gram Panchayat. 

The timeline for wage payment is typically within 15 days of work completion. 

The Ministry of Rural Development is the implementing authority for the scheme. 

State governments also play a crucial role in implementation. 

The scheme is funded by the central government and state governments in a 75:25 ratio.
