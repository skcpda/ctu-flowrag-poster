The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme is a flagship program of the Government of India. 

It was launched in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The main objective is to provide at least 100 days of guaranteed wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme aims to improve the purchasing power of rural households and reduce poverty. 

It also focuses on creating durable assets in rural areas and enhancing the livelihood security of rural communities. 

Eligible beneficiaries include every rural household that applies for employment under the scheme. 

The application process involves submitting a job card application at the local Gram Panchayat. 

Once the application is approved a job card is issued to the household. 

The scheme provides wage employment for various types of work including construction of roads and irrigation facilities. 

Wages are paid according to the minimum wage rates set by the state government. 

The implementation of the scheme is overseen by the Ministry of Rural Development. 

State governments are responsible for the execution of the scheme at the local level. 

The scheme is monitored through a decentralized structure involving local self-governments. 

The frequency of employment is demand-driven based on the applications received. 

Payments are made directly to the bank accounts of the beneficiaries. 

The scheme has provisions for social audits to ensure transparency and accountability.
