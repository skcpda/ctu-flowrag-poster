Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme (MGNREGS) is a social security measure implemented in India. 

It was launched in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The primary objective is to provide at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme aims to create durable assets and improve the livelihood security of rural households. 

Benefits include guaranteed wage employment and the creation of public works such as roads and irrigation facilities. 

The assistance provided includes minimum wage payment for the work done under the scheme. 

Eligibility is extended to every rural household that applies for employment under the scheme. 

The application process involves submitting a demand for work to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The timeline for wage payment is typically within 15 days of the completion of work. 

The implementing authority is the Ministry of Rural Development at the central level. 

State governments also play a crucial role in the implementation of the scheme. 

MGNREGS is funded by the central government with contributions from state governments. 

The scheme is monitored through a decentralized system involving local bodies. 

Regular audits and social audits are conducted to ensure transparency and accountability.
