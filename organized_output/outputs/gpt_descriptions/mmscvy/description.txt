Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act is also known as MGNREGA. 

It was enacted in 2005 to provide a legal guarantee for at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The objective is to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The scheme aims to provide social security and improve the economic condition of rural households. 

Benefits include guaranteed wage employment and the creation of durable assets in rural areas. 

Assistance is provided in the form of wages for unskilled manual work. 

Eligibility is limited to adult members of rural households. 

Households must apply for a job card to be eligible for employment under the scheme. 

The application process involves submitting a request for a job card at the local Gram Panchayat. 

The timeline for employment is within 15 days of the application for work. 

The implementing authority is the Ministry of Rural Development at the central level. 

State governments also play a crucial role in the implementation of the scheme. 

Funds are allocated to states based on the number of job cards issued and demand for work. 

The scheme is monitored through a decentralized structure involving local bodies.
