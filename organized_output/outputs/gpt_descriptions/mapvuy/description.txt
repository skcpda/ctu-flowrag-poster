The MAPVY (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act) scheme is a significant initiative by the Government of India. 

It aims to provide a legal guarantee for at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme was launched in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

It seeks to create durable assets and strengthen the livelihood resource base of rural households. 

The objectives of MAPVY include providing employment to rural households. 

It aims to empower rural communities by ensuring their right to work. 

The scheme also focuses on enhancing the livelihood security of the rural poor. 

Benefits under the MAPVY scheme include guaranteed wage employment. 

Wages are paid at the minimum wage rate as determined by the respective state governments. 

The scheme also provides for the creation of durable assets such as roads and irrigation facilities. 

This contributes to the overall development of rural infrastructure. 

Eligibility for the MAPVY scheme includes all rural households. 

Every household can apply for the scheme regardless of their economic status. 

The application process involves submitting a request for work to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The Gram Panchayat is responsible for registering the household under the scheme. 

Once registered the household can demand work under the scheme. 

The timeline for the application process is continuous throughout the year. 

There is no specific deadline for applying for the scheme. 

The implementing authorities for the MAPVY scheme include the Ministry of Rural Development. 

State governments are also responsible for the implementation at the local level. 

The scheme is monitored through a decentralized structure involving local bodies. 

The funds for the scheme are allocated by the central government to the states. 

States are responsible for the timely disbursement of wages to the workers.
