The National Food Security Mission (NFSM) is a government initiative aimed at enhancing food security in India. 

It was launched in 2007 to increase the production of rice wheat pulses and coarse cereals. 

The objective is to ensure availability of food grains at affordable prices to the population. 

The scheme aims to increase the productivity of these crops through improved agricultural practices and technology. 

Benefits include financial assistance for seeds fertilizers and other inputs. 

Farmers receive training and support for adopting better farming techniques. 

Eligibility is primarily focused on farmers engaged in the cultivation of rice wheat pulses and coarse cereals. 

The application process involves registration through state agricultural departments. 

Farmers may need to provide proof of land ownership and other relevant documents. 

The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare. 

It works in collaboration with state governments and agricultural universities. 

The NFSM operates on an annual basis with periodic reviews and updates. 

Funding is provided by the central government with contributions from state governments. 

The mission also emphasizes the importance of sustainable agricultural practices. 

It aims to enhance the income of farmers and improve their livelihoods. 

The NFSM promotes the use of high-yielding varieties of seeds and modern agricultural techniques. 

It also focuses on improving irrigation facilities and soil health. 

The scheme has provisions for monitoring and evaluation to assess its impact on food security.
