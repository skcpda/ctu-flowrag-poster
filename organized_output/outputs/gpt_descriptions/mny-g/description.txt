The MNY-G scheme stands for the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act. 

It was launched in 2005 to provide a legal guarantee for at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The main objective is to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The scheme aims to create durable assets and strengthen the livelihood resource base of rural households. 

Benefits include the provision of at least 100 days of wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

Wages are paid at the minimum wage rate determined by the state government. 

Eligibility is limited to adult members of rural households who are willing to do unskilled manual work. 

The application process involves submitting a demand for work to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The Gram Panchayat is responsible for issuing a job card to eligible households. 

The timeline for employment is based on the demand raised by the households. 

The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development at the national level. 

State governments are responsible for the execution and monitoring of the scheme at the state and local levels. 

The scheme also emphasizes the participation of women and marginalized communities. 

It includes provisions for social audits to ensure transparency and accountability.
