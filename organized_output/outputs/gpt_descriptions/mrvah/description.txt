The MRVAH scheme stands for the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act. 

It aims to provide at least 100 days of guaranteed wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme seeks to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

It also aims to create durable assets and strengthen the livelihood resource base of the rural poor. 

Benefits include wage employment for unskilled manual work. 

The scheme provides a legal guarantee for at least 100 days of unskilled work in a financial year. 

Eligibility is extended to every rural household. 

All adult members of the household are eligible to apply for work under the scheme. 

The application process involves submitting a job card application to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The Gram Panchayat is responsible for issuing job cards to eligible households. 

The scheme operates on a continuous basis throughout the year. 

The Ministry of Rural Development is the implementing authority for the MRVAH scheme. 

State governments also play a crucial role in the implementation at the local level. 

The scheme is funded by the central government and state governments in a cost-sharing ratio.
