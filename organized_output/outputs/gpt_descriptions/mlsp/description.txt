The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) is a significant welfare scheme in India. 

It was enacted in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas. 

The primary objective is to provide at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme aims to improve livelihood security and create durable assets in rural areas. 

Benefits include guaranteed employment and a daily wage for unskilled labor. 

The wage rate is determined by the state government and is revised periodically. 

Eligibility is extended to every rural household that demands work under the scheme. 

The application process involves submitting a job card application to the local Gram Panchayat. 

Once the job card is issued, households can request work as per their needs. 

The scheme operates throughout the year. 

The implementing authority is the Ministry of Rural Development at the central level. 

State governments are responsible for the execution and monitoring of the scheme at the state level. 

MGNREGA also emphasizes the participation of women and marginalized communities. 

It includes provisions for the payment of wages within 15 days of work completion. 

The scheme has provisions for grievance redressal to address issues faced by beneficiaries. 

MGNREGA is a crucial component of rural development and poverty alleviation strategies in India.
