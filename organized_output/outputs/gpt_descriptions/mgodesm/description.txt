The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act is a social security measure in India. 

It aims to enhance livelihood security in rural areas by providing at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

The scheme seeks to improve the purchasing power of rural households and reduce poverty. 

It also aims to create durable assets in rural areas and enhance the livelihood resource base of the rural poor. 

Eligible beneficiaries include every rural household whose adult members volunteer to do unskilled manual work. 

The application process involves submitting a written request for work to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The timeline for employment is based on the demand for work and the availability of resources. 

The Ministry of Rural Development is the primary implementing authority for this scheme. 

State governments and local bodies also play a significant role in the implementation. 

The scheme is funded by the central government and state governments in a cost-sharing ratio. 

Regular monitoring and evaluation are conducted to ensure effective implementation and transparency.
