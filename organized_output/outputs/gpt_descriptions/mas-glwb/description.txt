**Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA)**

MGNREGA was enacted in 2005 to enhance livelihood security in rural areas of India. 

The scheme aims to provide at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment in a financial year to every rural household. 

It seeks to create durable assets in rural areas and enhance the livelihood security of the rural population. 

The primary objective is to reduce poverty by providing employment opportunities and improving rural infrastructure. 

The scheme also aims to empower marginalized sections of society, particularly women and Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. 

Under MGNREGA, the government guarantees wage employment to rural households. 

The wages are paid at the minimum wage rate as determined by the state government. 

The scheme promotes the use of local resources and skills for the development of rural areas. 

It also encourages the participation of women in the workforce by ensuring that at least one-third of the total beneficiaries are women. 

The benefits of MGNREGA include the provision of employment, income security, and the creation of community assets. 

Households can apply for work under MGNREGA by submitting an application to the local Gram Panchayat. 

The application can be made at any time during the year. 

Once the application is received, the Gram Panchayat is required to provide employment within 15 days. 

If employment is not provided within this period, the applicants are entitled to an unemployment allowance. 

The implementing authorities for MGNREGA include the Ministry of Rural Development at the central level. 

State governments are responsible for the implementation of the scheme at the state and local levels. 

The Gram Panchayat plays a crucial role in the execution of the scheme by planning and implementing projects. 

MGNREGA is funded by the central government and state governments in a 75:25 ratio. 

The scheme is monitored through a multi-tier system to ensure transparency and accountability. 

MGNREGA also promotes social audits to involve the community in the monitoring process. 

The scheme has provisions for grievance redressal to address the issues faced by the beneficiaries.
